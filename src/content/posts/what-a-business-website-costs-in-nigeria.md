Ask three agencies to quote for "a company website" and you will get three
numbers that look unrelated. That is not usually dishonesty. It is that
"website" describes an outcome, not a scope, and each quote silently assumes a
different one.

## Where the money actually goes

A website's cost is mostly decided by four things, and none of them is the
number of pages.

**Who writes the content.** This is the single largest hidden cost. If you
supply finished text and images, the build is straightforward. If the agency
has to interview you, draft the copy, and chase you for approval, that is
several days of work that has nothing to do with code.

**Whether you need to edit it yourself.** A site whose text is fixed at build
time is quicker and cheaper to produce than one with an admin panel behind it.
The admin panel is worth paying for if you will genuinely use it — and pure
overhead if you will ring us to change a phone number once a year.

**What it has to connect to.** A brochure site stands alone. The moment it must
take payments, check stock, sync to an accounting package, or log in customers,
you are commissioning software, and software is priced differently.

**How much traffic it must survive.** Most Nigerian SMEs do not need to plan for
a hundred thousand concurrent visitors, and paying for that architecture up
front is money set on fire.

## Three questions that expose a bad quote

Ask any prospective developer these, and the answers will tell you more than
the price will.

1. **Who owns the code and the domain when we are finished?** The answer should
   be "you", without hesitation. If the domain is registered in the agency's
   name, changing supplier later becomes a negotiation.
2. **What happens after launch, and what does it cost?** A quote that stops at
   launch is not a full price. Hosting, renewals, and updates continue.
3. **Can I see the site on a mid-range Android phone over mobile data?** Not on
   the agency's laptop over office wifi. Most of your customers are on the
   former, and a site that only feels fast on the latter is not fast.

If a quote is far below everything else you have been offered, it is worth
asking which of the four cost drivers above it has quietly excluded. Usually it
is the content, and usually the project stalls there.
