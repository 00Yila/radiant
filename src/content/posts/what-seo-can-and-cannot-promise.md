If someone guarantees you the top result on Google, they are promising an
outcome they do not own. Nobody outside Google sets the ranking, the algorithm
changes continuously, and your competitors are working on the same thing. A
guarantee is a sales tactic, not a plan.

That does not make search work unbuyable. It means you should know which part
you are buying.

## What is genuinely within our control

**Technical foundations.** Whether a page loads quickly on a mid-range Android
phone over mobile data. Whether search engines can read and index it. Whether
it declares proper titles, descriptions and structured data. This is not
optional extra work — every site we build gets it, and it is why we do not sell
"SEO" as a separate line on a new build.

**Local presence.** A correctly configured Google Business Profile, consistent
name, address and phone across directories, and pages that actually mention the
areas you serve. For a business trading in Abuja, this moves the needle harder
than almost anything else.

**Content that answers real questions.** The pages that earn search traffic are
usually the ones answering something a customer typed at eleven at night. Our
own most-read pages are the ones about pricing and timelines, because those are
the questions people are actually asking.

## What nobody can control

- **Position one for a competitive term, on a date.** Not sellable, not
  honestly.
- **How fast results arrive.** Local terms can shift within weeks. Competitive
  national terms take months, and sometimes do not come at all.
- **Algorithm updates.** Google changes ranking behaviour several times a year.
  A site built on genuine substance survives these; one built on tricks does
  not.

## How to read an SEO proposal

Look for what is being measured. A proposal reporting **rankings** alone is easy
to flatter — pick obscure enough terms and everything looks like position three.
A proposal reporting **enquiries** is harder to fake and is the number your
business actually runs on.

Ask what happens if you stop. Honest search work leaves you with assets you keep:
faster pages, better content, a cleaned-up profile. If stopping means everything
vanishes next month, you were renting a position, not building one.
